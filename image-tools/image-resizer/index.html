Crop to fill, fit with border or stretch?

When a photo’s shape doesn’t match the new size — a landscape photo going into a 1080×1080 square, say — something has to give.

  • Crop to fill scales the photo to cover the whole frame and trims the edges evenly. Nothing is distorted and there are no bars. Best for social posts and thumbnails.
  • Fit with border keeps the entire photo and fills the leftover space with a colour you choose. Best when nothing may be cut off, such as documents and product shots.
  • Stretch forces the photo to the exact size, distorting it if the shapes differ. Use it only when the shapes are already almost the same.

Does resizing reduce quality?

Making an image smaller keeps it looking sharp — the tool downscales in several steps to avoid jagged edges. Making an image larger cannot add detail that was never captured: enlarging a small photo by more than about 150% makes it look soft. For the best result, start from the largest original you have.

To make files smaller without changing their dimensions, use the Image Compressor instead — or resize first and then compress.

Pixels, DPI and print size

Screens only care about pixels. For printing, divide the pixel size by the print resolution: at 300 DPI (photo quality), a 1800 × 1200 px image prints at 6 × 4 inches, and a 3000 × 2400 px image at 10 × 8 inches. For posters viewed from a distance, 150 DPI is usually enough.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I resize an image without stretching it?

Keep proportions locked and enter only a width or a height — the other side is calculated automatically. For an exact size with a different shape, use Crop to fill or Fit with border instead of stretching.

Can I resize many photos at once?

Yes. Add as many images as you like; every one is resized with the same settings, and you can download them all together as a ZIP file.

Are my photos uploaded?

No. Resizing happens entirely in your browser, so your photos never leave your device.

What size should an Instagram post be?

1080 × 1080 px for a square post or 1080 × 1350 px for a portrait (4:5) post. Stories and Reels use 1080 × 1920 px. All three are one-click presets here.

Will resizing reduce the file size?

Making an image smaller in pixels almost always makes the file smaller too. For precise control over file size, such as hitting 100 KB, use the Image Compressor.

Can I enlarge a small image?

Yes, by entering a bigger size or a percentage above 100%. The image gets bigger, but it cannot gain detail, so large enlargements look soft.

Which format should I save in?

JPG for photos that must work everywhere, WebP for smaller files on websites, and PNG for screenshots, graphics or images with transparency.

Does it keep my photo the right way up?

Yes. Phone photos are rotated according to their EXIF orientation before resizing, and the saved file is always upright.
