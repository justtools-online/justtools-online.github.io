### How to calculate your EMI

### Guide

#### What is an EMI?

An EMI (equated monthly instalment) is the fixed amount you pay your lender every month until a loan is repaid. Each EMI covers that month’s interest on the outstanding balance, and the rest reduces the principal. Because the balance falls over time, the interest part of each EMI shrinks and the principal part grows — even though the EMI itself stays the same.

#### The EMI formula

**EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1)**

Here **P** is the loan amount, **r** is the monthly interest rate (the annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and **n** is the number of monthly payments. For ₹10,00,000 at 10% a year for 20 years: r = 10 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.008333 and n = 240, which gives an EMI of **₹9,650**. Over 20 years you would repay ₹23,16,052, of which ₹13,16,052 is interest.

#### How prepayment saves money

Any amount you pay on top of the EMI goes straight to the principal, so every later month charges interest on a smaller balance. Take a ₹50 lakh home loan at 8.5% for 20 years: the EMI is ₹43,391 and the total interest ₹54,13,879. Paying just **₹5,000 extra every month** closes the loan in 15 years 7 months instead of 20 and saves about **₹13.9 lakh** in interest.

Prepayments save the most early in the loan, when the balance — and so the interest — is highest. Many lenders also let you keep the tenure and reduce the EMI instead; this calculator shows the “shorter tenure” option, which saves more interest.

#### How to lower your EMI or total interest

- **Make a bigger down payment.** Every rupee you don’t borrow saves interest for the whole tenure.
- **Compare interest rates.** On a ₹50 lakh, 20-year loan, 0.5% lower rate cuts the EMI by roughly ₹1,600 a month.
- **Choose the shortest tenure you can afford.** A longer tenure lowers the EMI but multiplies the total interest.
- **Prepay whenever you can,** especially in the first years. Check your loan agreement for any prepayment charges first.
- **Keep your EMIs affordable.** A common rule of thumb is to keep all EMIs together below 40–50% of your monthly take-home income.

### FAQ

**How is EMI calculated?**
With the reducing-balance formula EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n the number of months. This calculator applies it exactly and builds the full schedule from it.

**Why is my bank’s EMI slightly different?**
Lenders may round differently, count interest from the disbursal date, or add processing fees and insurance to the loan. The formula is the same, so the difference is normally small.

**Does a longer tenure save money?**
No. A longer tenure lowers the EMI but increases the total interest. A ₹10 lakh loan at 10% costs ₹2,74,823 in interest over 5 years but ₹13,16,052 over 20 years.

**Is it better to reduce the EMI or the tenure after a prepayment?**
Reducing the tenure saves more interest; reducing the EMI eases your monthly budget. If you can comfortably keep paying the same EMI, choose the shorter tenure.

**Can I calculate EMI for a car, personal or education loan?**
Yes. All of these are standard reducing-balance loans. Enter the amount, rate and tenure from your offer — or tap one of the examples to start.

**Does this work for a flat-rate loan?**
No. Some personal and vehicle loans quote a “flat” rate charged on the original amount for the whole tenure, which costs much more than the same reducing rate. Ask the lender for the reducing-balance rate (or APR) and enter that.

**Can I download the repayment schedule?**
Yes. Click **CSV** above the schedule to download every month’s payment, principal, interest, extra payment and balance for Excel or Google Sheets.

**Is my data saved?**
No. The calculations run in your browser, and nothing you enter is stored or sent anywhere.

### At a glance

- BMI = weight in kg ÷ (height in m)². In pounds and inches the same formula is 703 × lb ÷ in².
- WHO adult categories: below **18.5** underweight, **18.5–24.9** healthy, **25–29.9** overweight and **30 or more** obesity (classes I, II and III from 30, 35 and 40).

- **Your healthy weight range**: See the exact weight range that gives a healthy BMI at your height, and the weight for every other category too.
- **Waist-to-height ratio**: Add your waist size to check belly fat — a better guide to heart and diabetes risk than BMI alone.

### How to calculate your BMI

1. **Enter your height** — Type your height in feet and inches, or switch to centimetres.
2. **Enter your weight** — Type your weight in kilograms, or switch to pounds. Weigh yourself in the morning, in light clothes, for the most consistent number.
3. **Choose the categories** — Keep **WHO (international)**, or pick **Asian / Indian** if you are of South Asian, Chinese or other Asian descent. Add your age and waist size for more context.
4. **Read your result** — See your BMI and category on the scale, your healthy weight range and how far you are from it.

### Guide

#### BMI chart for adults

| Category | WHO (international) | Asian / Indian |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Underweight | Below 18.5 | Below 18.5 |
| Healthy weight | 18.5 – 24.9 | 18.5 – 22.9 |
| Overweight | 25.0 – 29.9 | 23.0 – 24.9 |
| Obesity | 30.0 or more | 25.0 or more |

#### How BMI is calculated

Divide your weight in kilograms by your height in metres squared. For someone who weighs 68 kg and is 1.70 m tall: 68 ÷ (1.70 × 1.70) = 68 ÷ 2.89 = **23.5**.

In pounds and inches, multiply by 703: for 150 lb and 5 ft 5 in (65 in), 703 × 150 ÷ 65² = 105,450 ÷ 4,225 = **25.0**. The calculator uses exact unit conversions, so both methods agree.

#### Why Asians have lower BMI cut-offs

At the same BMI, people of South Asian descent tend to carry more body fat, especially around the waist, and develop type 2 diabetes and heart disease at a lower BMI than people of European descent.

That is why the WHO Asia-Pacific classification and Indian consensus guidelines treat a BMI of **23** as overweight and **25** as obesity. If you are of Asian descent, a BMI of 24 is a reason to act, even though it is “healthy” on the international scale.

#### What BMI doesn’t tell you

- **Muscle vs fat:** muscular people and athletes can have a high BMI without excess fat.
- **Where fat sits:** fat around the waist carries more risk. Your waist should be less than half your height.
- **Older adults:** muscle is lost with age, so body fat can be higher than BMI suggests.
- **Pregnancy:** BMI isn’t valid during pregnancy; use your pre-pregnancy weight when a doctor asks for it.
- **Children and teens:** BMI is compared with others of the same age and sex on growth charts rather than with the adult categories.

### FAQ

**What is a healthy BMI?**
For adults, **18.5 to 24.9** on the WHO scale. For people of Asian descent, including Indians, the recommended healthy range is **18.5 to 22.9**.

**Which cut-offs should I use if I’m Indian?**
The **Asian / Indian** cut-offs. Indian consensus guidelines count a BMI of 23–24.9 as overweight and 25 or more as obesity, because health risks rise at a lower BMI in South Asians.

**How is BMI calculated?**
Weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. For example, 68 kg ÷ (1.70 m)² = 23.5. In imperial units, BMI = 703 × weight in pounds ÷ height in inches squared.

**Is BMI accurate?**
It is a quick screening measure that works well for most adults, but it can’t tell fat from muscle or show where fat is stored. Pair it with your waist-to-height ratio, and speak to a doctor about your overall health.

**Is BMI different for men and women?**
No. Adults use the same formula and categories for both sexes, although women naturally carry more body fat than men at the same BMI.

**Can I use this calculator for children?**
The BMI number is calculated the same way, but children and teenagers under 18 must be assessed on age- and sex-specific growth charts, not the adult categories. Enter an age under 18 and the calculator will tell you so.

**What is BMI Prime?**
Your BMI divided by the upper limit of the healthy range (25, or 23 on the Asian scale). A value above 1.00 means overweight; 1.20 means 20% above the limit.

**What is a healthy waist-to-height ratio?**
Keep your waist to less than half your height: a ratio of 0.4–0.49 is healthy, 0.5–0.59 means increased health risk and 0.6 or more means high risk (NICE, 2022).

**Is my data saved?**
No. The calculation runs in your browser, and your height, weight and age are never sent anywhere.

#### Order of operations

The calculator follows the standard order: brackets first, then functions and factorials, powers (right to left, so 2^3^2 = 2^9), multiplication and division from left to right, and finally addition and subtraction. A minus sign in front applies after powers, so **−2² = −4** while **(−2)² = 4**.

**What is the difference between log and ln?**
**log** is the base-10 logarithm (log 1000 = 3) and **ln** is the natural logarithm to base e (ln e = 1). For another base type log(x, base), for example log(8, 2) = 3.

**How does the % key work?**
After + or −, a percentage is taken of the first number: 200 + 10% = 220 and 200 − 10% = 180. Otherwise it divides by 100: 200 × 10% = 20, and 50% = 0.5.

### Guide

#### FD formula

For a cumulative FD compounded quarterly: **A = P × (1 + r/4)^(4t)**, where P is the deposit, r the yearly rate as a decimal and t the tenure in years. ₹1,00,000 at 7% for 5 years grows to 1,00,000 × 1.0175²⁰ = **₹1,41,478**.

Because interest is added every quarter, a 7% FD really earns **7.19% a year** — the effective annual yield. When the tenure is not a whole number of quarters, the leftover days earn simple interest on the balance.

A payout FD does not compound: a quarterly payout is P × r/4. Monthly payouts are paid at a slightly discounted rate because you receive the money earlier.

#### Tax on FD interest in India

- FD interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate, every year, even on a cumulative FD.
- A bank deducts **10% TDS** when your total interest with it crosses **₹50,000** in a financial year (**₹1,00,000** for senior citizens) — 20% if it has no PAN.
- If your total income is below the taxable limit, submit **Form 15G** (or **15H** for senior citizens) to avoid TDS.
- Tax-saving FDs have a 5-year lock-in and no premature withdrawal.
- Deposits are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, including interest.

### FAQ

**How is FD interest calculated?**
Indian banks compound FD interest every quarter: A = P × (1 + r/4)^(4t). For FDs under six months most banks pay simple interest — choose **Simple interest** under Compounding for those.

**How much interest will I get on ₹1 lakh for 1 year?**
At 7% with quarterly compounding, ₹1,00,000 becomes ₹1,07,186 — ₹7,186 of interest. Enter your bank’s rate to see your own figure.

**What is the monthly interest on a ₹10 lakh FD?**
Choose **Monthly payout**. At 7% it is about ₹5,800 a month — slightly less than 7% ÷ 12 because banks discount monthly payouts.

**Do senior citizens get a higher FD rate?**

Multiply each course’s credits by its grade points, add those up, and divide by the total credits. Five courses worth 15 credits with 134 credit points give 134 ÷ 15 = **8.93**.

**How do I calculate CGPA from SGPA?**
Multiply each semester’s SGPA by its credits, add the results, and divide by the total credits. If every semester has the same credits, the CGPA is simply the average of the SGPAs.

**How do I convert CGPA to percentage?**

CBSE set 9.5 as the factor for the CGPA it printed on Class 10 marksheets, so a perfect 10 CGPA corresponds to 95%. Many colleges borrowed the same factor, but it is not a universal rule.

**What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?**
An unweighted GPA treats every course the same, on a 4.0 scale. A weighted GPA adds 0.5 for Honors and 1.0 for AP or IB courses to reward harder classes, so it can exceed 4.0.

#### CGST, SGST and IGST

- **CGST** (central GST) and **SGST** (state GST) apply together to a sale within one state. Each is half the rate: 18% GST is 9% CGST + 9% SGST.
- **UTGST** replaces SGST in union territories without a legislature, such as Chandigarh and Ladakh.
- **IGST** (integrated GST) applies to a sale from one state to another, and to imports. It is the full rate as one tax: 18% IGST.
- The total tax is the same either way — only where the money goes differs.

#### What changed with GST 2.0

The GST Council’s 56th meeting on 3 September 2025 replaced the four slabs of 5%, 12%, 18% and 28% with **5% and 18%**, plus **40%** for a few goods. Most 12% items moved to 5%, and most 28% items — air conditioners, large TVs, cement, small cars, motorcycles up to 350 cc — moved to 18%. The new rates apply from **22 September 2025**.

The **compensation cess** on cars, aerated drinks and coal ended on the same day, which is why larger cars are now a flat 40% instead of 28% plus cess. Cigarettes, pan masala and other tobacco products moved to 40% on **1 February 2026**, charged on the printed retail price, with a separate excise duty or health cess on top.

#### Input tax credit

A GST-registered business does not pay the whole GST it collects. It deducts the GST it paid on its own purchases — the **input tax credit** — and pays the difference. Buy goods for ₹10,000 + ₹1,800 GST and sell them for ₹12,000 + ₹2,160 GST: the tax to pay is 2,160 − 1,800 = **₹360**, which is 18% of the ₹2,000 of value added.

**How do I calculate GST on an amount?**
Multiply the amount by the GST rate and divide by 100. For ₹5,000 at 18%: 5,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = ₹900 of GST, so the total is ₹5,900. Within a state that is ₹450 CGST + ₹450 SGST.

**How do I remove GST from a total?**
Divide the total by 1 + the rate: by **1.05** for 5%, **1.18** for 18%, **1.40** for 40%. A ₹5,900 bill at 18% is 5,900 ÷ 1.18 = ₹5,000 before GST, with ₹900 of tax. Choose **Remove GST** above to do it automatically.

**What are the GST rates in 2026?**
The main slabs are **5%** and **18%**, with **40%** on a short list of luxury and sin goods. Essentials are nil-rated, gold and silver are 3%, and diamonds 0.25% or 1.5%. The 12% and 28% slabs ended on 22 September 2025.

**Is 12% or 28% GST still charged?**
Not on new sales. Those slabs were abolished on 22 September 2025 — tobacco products stayed at 28% plus cess until 1 February 2026 and are now 40%. Both rates are still offered here so you can check older invoices.

**What is the difference between CGST, SGST and IGST?**
CGST and SGST are charged together, half the rate each, when buyer and seller are in the same state. IGST is charged alone, at the full rate, when they are in different states or the goods are imported.

**What is the GST on mobile phones, gold and cars?**
Mobile phones are **18%**. Gold and silver jewellery is **3%**, with 5% on making charges. Small cars are **18%**; larger cars and SUVs are **40%**; electric vehicles are 5%. Search the rate finder above for other items.

Each tax is rounded to the nearest paisa, and CGST and SGST are always equal — so when GST is taken out of a price within a state, the value before tax can sit one paisa below price ÷ (1 + rate). ### Guide

#### Avalanche or snowball?

Both methods pay every loan its normal payment and send any extra money to one loan at a time. **Avalanche** targets the highest interest rate, which is mathematically the cheapest. **Snowball** targets the smallest balance, so the first loan disappears sooner – a boost that helps some people keep going. On the example below the difference is small, so the best method is the one you will stick to.

#### When refinancing makes sense

- You have **private loans** at a high rate and can qualify for a clearly lower one – the example’s $15,000 private loan at 9% costs $7,802 in interest over 10 years, but $4,535 at 5.5%.
- You will **keep or shorten the term**: stretching the term lowers the payment but can raise the total interest even at a lower rate.
- Any **fees** are repaid by the lower payments well before the loan ends.
- You do **not** need federal benefits – income-driven repayment, Public Service Loan Forgiveness, generous deferment and forbearance. Refinancing federal loans privately ends them for good.

### FAQ

**How do I pay off student loans faster?**
Pay more than the minimum and direct the extra to one loan at a time – the highest-rate loan saves the most interest. When that loan is gone, add its payment to the next. Ask your servicer to apply extra payments to principal on the loan you choose, not to future payments.

**Is it better to pay off the highest interest or the smallest balance first?**
Highest interest first (avalanche) always costs least. Smallest balance first (snowball) gives earlier wins. Try both in the calculator: with similar rates the difference is often a few hundred dollars.

**Should I refinance my federal student loans?**
Only after weighing what you give up. A private refinance can lower the rate, but the loans lose federal protections such as income-driven repayment, Public Service Loan Forgiveness and federal deferment and forbearance, and that cannot be reversed. Many borrowers refinance only their private loans.

**Does refinancing always save money?**
No. A lower rate over a longer term can cost more in total interest, and fees add to the cost. The calculator compares the lifetime cost of keeping your loans with the offer, fees included.

**How is student loan interest calculated?**
Most US student loans charge simple daily interest; this calculator uses the monthly equivalent, APR ÷ 12 on the balance, which gives very close results. Your servicer’s figures may differ by small amounts because of day counts and payment dates.

**How is a mortgage payment calculated?**

**How do I avoid or get rid of PMI?**
Put down at least 20% on a conventional loan. If you put down less, you can ask to cancel PMI once the balance is 80% of the original value – sooner if you pay extra principal – and it ends by itself at 78%. A big rise in value can also help, but lenders usually require an appraisal for that.

**Are extra mortgage payments worth it?**
Every extra dollar of principal saves the loan’s interest rate on it for the rest of the loan. $200 a month more on a $320,000 loan at 6.5% saves about $105,000 and 6½ years. Check that your loan has no prepayment penalty and ask your servicer to apply extra payments to principal.

**What property tax rate should I use?**
Use the rate or yearly bill for the specific home: listings and county assessor websites show the current tax. Rates vary a lot between states and counties, and taxes are based on the assessed value, which can differ from the price.

### FAQ

**How do I calculate my car payment?**
Work out the amount financed – price, minus rebate, trade-in equity and down payment, plus any tax and fees you finance – then apply P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where r is the APR ÷ 12 and n the number of months. $29,690 at 6.5% for 60 months is $580.92.

**Does a trade-in reduce sales tax?**
In most states, yes: you pay sales tax only on the price minus the trade-in. California, Hawaii, Virginia and Washington DC tax the full price, and Michigan caps the credit. Check your state’s rules and set the switch to match.

**What happens if I owe more than my trade-in is worth?**
That negative equity is usually added to the new loan, so you pay interest on it and start the new loan upside down. Paying it off in cash, waiting until you have equity, or choosing a cheaper car avoids the trap.

**Is 0% APR or cash back better?**
It depends on the rebate, your own rate and the term. Enter both offers in **Low APR or cash back?** – the one with the lower total cost wins. Large rebates often beat a low rate on shorter loans; low rates win on longer ones.

**What is the out-the-door price?**
The full amount to buy the car: price minus rebates, plus sales tax and fees. Negotiating on the out-the-door price stops add-ons and fees from creeping in after you agree a price.
