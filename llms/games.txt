### Guide

#### The rules

Two players take turns placing their mark — X or O — in an empty square of a 3×3 grid. The first player to get three of their marks in a row, column or diagonal wins. If all nine squares fill up with no line, the game is a draw.

The game is also called **noughts and crosses** in Britain and **zero kata** in India. On bigger boards the idea is the same, but you need the number in a row shown under the board.

#### How to never lose at 3×3

- No first move wins by force: with best play from both sides, every opening ends in a draw.
- If your opponent starts in a **corner**, take the **centre** — every other reply loses against perfect play.
- If they start in the **centre**, take a **corner**. Answering on an edge loses.
- If they start on an **edge**, take the centre, a corner next to their mark, or the edge opposite it.
- On every turn, first look for a winning move, then block any two-in-a-row of theirs.
- Win with a **fork**: a move that makes two lines of two at once, so your opponent can only block one.

### Guide

#### Chances of streaks

| Event | Chance |
| --- | --- |
| Heads on one flip | 1 in 2 (50%) |
| Heads three times in a row | 1 in 8 (12.5%) |
| Heads five times in a row | 1 in 32 (3.1%) |
| Heads ten times in a row | 1 in 1,024 (0.1%) |
| Three heads in a row somewhere in 10 flips | 50.8% |
| Six heads in a row somewhere in 100 flips | 54.6% |

#### Is the next flip “due”?

No. A fair coin has no memory: after five heads in a row, the next flip is still exactly 50/50. Believing otherwise is called the **gambler’s fallacy**. What does happen is that over thousands of flips the *percentage* of heads drifts towards 50% — try the 1,000-flip button to see it.

### FAQ

**Is this coin flip really random?**
Yes. Each result comes from your browser’s cryptographic random number generator (crypto.getRandomValues), so heads and tails are equally likely and no flip depends on the one before.

**Can I use it for a cricket toss?**
Yes. Choose **Bat / Bowl** and let the coin decide — or keep Heads/Tails and have the captains call it.

**Is a real coin toss 50/50?**
Almost. A 2023 study by Bartoš and colleagues, with 350,757 real flips, found coins land on the side they started on slightly more often — about 50.8% of the time. This virtual coin has no starting side, so it is exactly 50/50.

**Can I flip more than one coin?**
Yes, up to 10 at once — the result shows how many landed heads and tails. For quick experiments, add 10, 100 or 1,000 flips to the statistics.

**What does best of 3 mean?**
The first side to win two flips wins (three for best of 5, four for best of 7). It makes a decision feel fairer than a single toss.

### Guide

#### Minesweeper patterns worth knowing

| Pattern | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| A 1 with one hidden neighbour | That neighbour is a mine. |
| A number with all its mines flagged | Every other neighbour is safe — click the number to open them. |
| 1-1 along a wall | The cell beyond the second 1 is safe. |
| 1-2-1 along a wall | The mines are under the 1s; the cell under the 2 is safe. |
| 1-2-2-1 along a wall | The mines are under the two 2s. |

#### What is 3BV?

3BV (Bechtel’s Board Benchmark Value) is the smallest number of clicks that can clear a board: one click per open area plus one per number not on the edge of an open area. Dividing it by your time gives **3BV/s**, the fairest way to compare speed across boards.

**What does clicking a number do?**
If that number already has as many flags around it as its value, clicking it opens all its other neighbours. If a flag is wrong, you’ll set off a mine.

**Do hints count against my best time?**
Games where you used a hint still count as wins, but not for your best time or 3BV/s.

### How to play 2048

1. **Slide the tiles** — Swipe, or press an arrow key, to move every tile as far as it can go.
2. **Join equal numbers** — Two tiles with the same number merge into their sum: 2 + 2 = 4, 4 + 4 = 8…
3. **Plan ahead** — Keep your biggest tile in a corner and build the row next to it in order.
4. **Reach 2048** — Make a 2048 tile to win — or keep going for a higher score. The game ends when no move is left.

### Guide

#### Strategy that works

- **Pick a corner** for your biggest tile and keep it there — mostly use two directions (say left and down) and a third only when needed.
- **Keep the edge row in order**, largest to smallest away from the corner, so tiles can chain into each other.
- **Avoid the fourth direction**: pressing it pulls the big tile out of its corner and lets a small tile land behind it.
- **Think about where the new tile will appear** — leave space on the side away from your corner.

#### Scoring

Each merge adds the new tile’s value to your score: joining two 8s gives 16 points. Reaching 2048 usually means a score of about 20,000.
