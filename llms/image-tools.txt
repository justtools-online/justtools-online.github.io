### Guide

#### How does image compression work?

Photos from a phone or camera store far more detail than a screen can show. Lossy formats such as JPG, WebP and AVIF discard the detail your eye is least likely to notice — fine texture in busy areas, tiny colour shifts — and describe what remains more efficiently. The quality setting controls how aggressive that trade-off is.

PNG is lossless, so it has no quality setting in the same sense. For PNGs this tool reduces the number of distinct colours (colour quantisation) and then stores the result losslessly. Screenshots, logos and illustrations use relatively few colours, so they shrink dramatically with almost no visible change.

Everything is done with your browser’s built-in image encoders, so the output is a standard file that opens in any app.

#### JPG vs PNG vs WebP vs AVIF: which format should you use?

Rule of thumb: use **WebP** for anything going on a website, **JPG** when a form or older app insists on it, and **PNG** only for graphics with sharp edges, text or transparency.

| Format | Best for | Transparency | Compression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| JPG / JPEG | Photos that must open everywhere — email, forms, printing | No | Lossy; the universal baseline |
| WebP | Photos and graphics on websites | Yes | Lossy or lossless; typically 25–35% smaller than JPG at similar quality |
| AVIF | Websites that want the smallest possible files | Yes | Often around half the size of JPG, but slower to encode and not supported by every app |
| PNG | Screenshots, logos, text and flat graphics | Yes | Lossless; this tool adds optional colour reduction |

#### How to compress an image to 100 KB, 50 KB or 20 KB

Job portals, exam applications and government forms often cap uploads at a fixed size such as 20 KB, 50 KB or 100 KB. Switch the mode to **To target size**, pick a preset or type your own limit, and every image is compressed to the highest quality that still fits.

Rather than dropping to a blotchy, very low quality, the tool reduces the dimensions in small steps until the image fits, and labels the result so you know. When a form also specifies pixel dimensions, turn on **Resize large images** and enter them first — the tool then lowers quality as far as needed before it touches those dimensions.

Most forms expect a JPG file. If the form says JPG or JPEG, set the output format to **JPG** so the file type matches.

#### Recommended quality settings

- **85–90** — near-lossless. For portfolios and images you may edit again.
- **70–80** — the sweet spot for websites and sharing; most people cannot tell it apart from the original.
- **50–65** — noticeably smaller files for thumbnails, previews and chat apps.
- **Below 50** — only when size matters more than quality; blocky artefacts become visible up close.

#### Why compress images?

- **Faster websites.** Images are usually the heaviest part of a page. Smaller images improve Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), one of Google’s Core Web Vitals.
- **Upload limits.** Forms, marketplaces and content management systems reject files above a size limit.
- **Email and chat.** Gmail allows 25 MB of attachments per message, so smaller photos mean more of them per email.
- **Storage.** Compressing a photo library can free gigabytes on your phone or cloud drive.

Many online compressors upload your photos to their servers. This one does not: images are decoded, compressed and saved entirely inside your browser tab, and the page never sends them anywhere. You can check it yourself — load the page, switch off Wi-Fi, and compress an image. It still works.

#### Does resizing reduce quality?

**Making an image smaller** keeps it looking sharp — the tool downscales in several steps to avoid jagged edges. **Making an image larger** cannot add detail that was never captured: enlarging a small photo by more than about 150% makes it look soft. Screens only care about pixels. For printing, divide the pixel size by the print resolution: at 300 DPI (photo quality), a 1800 × 1200 px image prints at 6 × 4 inches, and a 3000 × 2400 px image at 10 × 8 inches. For posters viewed from a distance, 150 DPI is usually enough.

#### What is WebP?

WebP is an image format created by Google that supports both lossy and lossless compression, transparency and animation. The same picture is usually noticeably smaller as WebP than as JPG or PNG, which makes web pages load faster.

Every current browser displays WebP — Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Opera, and Safari on macOS Big Sur and iOS 14 or later — as do Android, Windows 10/11, macOS and most image editors.

#### Lossy or lossless WebP?

|  | Lossy WebP | Lossless WebP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best for | Photos and complex images | Logos, icons, screenshots, graphics with text |
| Replaces | JPG | PNG |
| Pixels | Slightly changed (invisible at good quality) | Identical to the original |
| Typical saving | 25–34% vs JPG | About 26% vs PNG |
| Transparency | Yes | Yes |

#### When not to use WebP

- **Email attachments and documents** — some email clients and older Office versions still handle JPG and PNG more reliably.

- **Very old software** — Windows 7 photo viewers and old editors may not open WebP.
- **Animated GIFs** — this tool converts the first frame only, into a still image.

#### Using WebP on your website

Serve WebP to every visitor today — all modern browsers support it. If you must support very old browsers, use a `<picture>` element with a WebP `<source>` and a JPG `<img>` fallback. Converting your largest images to WebP is one of the quickest ways to improve Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), a Core Web Vitals metric.

The catch is compatibility: many Windows PCs, Android phones, websites and upload forms can’t open HEIC. Converting to JPG makes the photos work everywhere.

| --- | --- | --- |

#### How to make your iPhone save JPG instead

Open **Settings → Camera → Formats** and choose **Most Compatible**. New photos are then saved as JPG. Existing HEIC photos stay HEIC — convert those here.

When you email a photo or share it to some apps, iOS may already convert it to JPG automatically. Photos copied from the phone to a computer over USB or cloud storage usually stay HEIC.

#### Opening HEIC on Windows

Windows 10 and 11 can display HEIC only after installing the HEIF Image Extensions and HEVC Video Extensions from the Microsoft Store, and the HEVC extension is a paid download. Converting to JPG avoids installing anything.

Blur faces, number plates, text or the background in any photo. Blur, pixelate or black out areas, then download it full size — free, private, no upload.

### At a glance

- Blurs **marked areas**, **everything except the marked areas** (background blur), or the **whole photo**.
- Three effects: smooth **Gaussian blur**, **pixelate** (mosaic) and a solid **black box** — with adjustable strength.
- Mark areas as **rectangles or ovals**; drag to move them, drag a corner to resize, and press Delete to remove one.
- The live preview matches the final image; the download is **full resolution** in the original format (JPG, PNG or WebP; HEIC becomes JPG).
- Location and camera data (EXIF) are not copied to the blurred image.
- Runs entirely in your browser — the photo is never uploaded.

### Features

- **Hide faces and plates**: Blur children’s faces, bystanders, car number plates and house numbers before posting photos online.
- **Censor screenshots**: Cover names, phone numbers, UPI IDs, OTPs and account details in chat or payment screenshots.
- **Background blur**: Mark the subject and choose “Everything else” for a portrait-style blurred background.
- **Blur, pixelate or black box**: Pick the look you want. For text that must stay secret, the black box can never be reversed.
- **Before and after**: Press and hold “Hold to see original” to compare with the untouched photo at any time.
- **Private and free**: No upload, no watermark, no sign-up, and full-size downloads.

### How to blur part of an image

1. **Open your photo** — Drop a JPG, PNG, WebP or HEIC photo onto the box, or paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V.
2. **Mark what to hide** — Drag over each face, plate or piece of text. Use **Oval** for faces. Move or resize areas by dragging.
3. **Choose the effect** — Pick **Blur**, **Pixelate** or **Black box** and set the strength. Choose **Everything else** to blur the background instead.
4. **Download** — Click **Download** to save the full-size blurred photo.

### Guide

#### Which effect should I use?

| Effect | Looks like | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blur | Soft and natural | Faces, backgrounds, bystanders |

#### Is blurred text really unreadable?

Not always. Light blur or small pixel blocks can leave enough of the shapes of letters and digits for software to guess them, especially for short numbers. For anything sensitive — Aadhaar, PAN, card or account numbers, passwords, OTPs — use **Black box**, or a strength of 60 or more so no detail remains.

For documents, a black box in [Redact PDF](https://justtools.online/pdf-tools/redact-pdf/) removes the text itself rather than only covering it.

### FAQ

**How do I blur a face in a photo?**
Open the photo, choose **Oval** as the area shape and drag over the face. Set the effect to **Blur** with a strength of 50 or more, then download.

**How do I blur the background of a photo?**
Mark the person or object you want to keep sharp, then choose **Everything else**. Everything outside the marked areas is blurred.

**Will the downloaded image be lower quality?**
The size stays exactly the same. JPG and WebP photos are saved at high quality (92); PNG stays lossless.

**Can someone un-blur my image?**
A strong blur or pixelation can’t be undone to reveal faces, but weakly blurred text can sometimes be guessed. Use Black box for secret text and numbers.

**Does it work on phones?**
Yes. Drag with your finger to mark areas. To scroll past the photo, switch the toggle above it to **Scroll page**.

**Is my photo uploaded?**
No. The photo is processed in your browser on your own device and never leaves it.

#### Tips for an accepted photo

Stand about a metre in front of a **plain white or very light wall**, facing a window or soft light so there are no shadows on your face or behind you. Look straight at the camera with a neutral expression and your mouth closed, eyes open and clearly visible. Avoid glasses and caps; religious headwear is usually allowed if the whole face is visible.

Ask someone else to take the photo from about 1.5 metres away at eye level — selfies distort the face. Use a recent photo (taken within the last six months), and always check the latest rules of the office you are applying to.

### FAQ

35 × 45 mm (3.5 cm wide, 4.5 cm tall) with a plain white background, the face covering most of the photo, taken from the front. Choose “India passport” and the tool sets this for you.

**What is the PAN card photo size?**
25 × 35 mm (2.5 × 3.5 cm), in colour with a white background. Choose “PAN card”, then download a single photo or a print sheet.

**How do I reduce a photo to 20 KB or 50 KB for an online form?**
Choose Online form, set the maximum size (such as 50 KB) and the width in pixels your form asks for, and download. The tool picks the highest quality that fits the limit.

**How many passport photos fit on a 4 × 6 photo print?**
Eight 35 × 45 mm photos fit on a 4 × 6 in (10 × 15 cm) print, with small gaps to cut along. Six 2 × 2 in photos fit on the same print.

**Can it change the background to white?**
Not automatically. Take the photo against a plain white or light wall; any empty edges around the photo are filled with white, light grey or light blue.

**Is my photo uploaded?**
No. Everything is done in your browser, so your photo never leaves your device.
