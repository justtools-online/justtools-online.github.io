### Guide

| Type | Pages included |

#### Why merge PDFs in your browser?

Most PDF websites upload your files to their servers, process them there and keep them for a while before deleting. For tax returns, contracts, medical reports or anything with personal data, that is an unnecessary risk.

This tool uses pdf-lib, a well-established open-source PDF library, running inside your browser tab. Your files are read from your disk, combined in memory and saved straight back to your downloads folder. You can load the page, disconnect from the internet and merge — it still works.

#### Common uses

- **Job and college applications:** combine a CV, cover letter and certificates into the single PDF a portal asks for.
- **Scanned documents:** join pages scanned one at a time into one file, in the right order.
- **Invoices and statements:** bundle a month of invoices or bank statements for your accountant.
- **Reports:** add a cover page or appendix to a report exported from Word or Google Docs.
- **Trimming:** keep only the pages you need from a long PDF before emailing it.

#### Tips for the best result

- Check the page count shown under each file before merging — it tells you the file was read correctly.
- If a PDF is password-protected, open it with the password and save a copy without one first.
- The merged file is roughly the sum of the pages you selected. To make it smaller for email, run it through a PDF compressor afterwards.
- Form fields and bookmarks from the original files are not guaranteed to carry over; page content always does.

**Original quality** keeps every JPG and PNG exactly as it is. A PDF of ten phone photos can therefore be 20–40 MB — perfect for archiving and printing.

**How do I make the PDF smaller?**
Choose **Smaller file** before converting. Large camera photos are resized to 2000 px and compressed, which usually cuts the size by 70–90%.

**Is there a limit on the number of images?**
No fixed limit. Very large batches are limited only by your device’s memory.

**Can I add more pages to an existing PDF?**
Convert the new images to a PDF here, then combine both files with [Merge PDF](https://justtools.online/pdf-tools/merge-pdf/).

### Guide

#### Two kinds of PDF password

A PDF can have an **open password** (also called the user password), which you must type before you can see anything, and a **permissions password** (owner password), which lets the file open normally but blocks printing, copying or editing.

For an open password, enter it here and the tool saves a copy without protection. For permissions-only files, no password is needed — the restrictions are removed as soon as you add the file.

#### Password formats for common Indian documents

| Document | Password format | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| e-Aadhaar (UIDAI) | First 4 letters of your name in CAPITALS + year of birth | `SURE1990` |
| ITR-V acknowledgement | PAN in lowercase + date of birth as DDMMYYYY | `abcde1234f01011990` |
| Bank and credit-card statements | Varies by bank — often part of your name with your date of birth, or your customer ID | See the email the statement came with |

#### Is it safe and legal to unlock a PDF?

Unlocking your own documents — statements, payslips, tax forms, ID copies — to store or share them more easily is routine and safe, especially when the file never leaves your device.

Only remove protection from documents you own or are authorised to use. Removing restrictions from someone else’s copyrighted document may break its terms of use. After unlocking, anyone with the file can open it, so store unlocked copies carefully.

#### Forgot the password?

This tool cannot recover a forgotten password, and modern AES-256 PDFs cannot practically be cracked. Ask the sender for the password or a new copy — banks and government portals let you download statements and documents again, and their emails usually explain the password format.

### FAQ

Yes. Add all the statements, enter the password once, and every file that uses it is unlocked. Download them together as a ZIP.

Yes. Only the encryption is removed; pages, text, images, links and the file’s quality are unchanged.

**Which encryption types are supported?**
All standard PDF encryption: AES-256 (Acrobat X and later), AES-128, and RC4 128-bit and 40-bit used by older software.

**I get “Wrong password” but I’m sure it’s right. What should I try?**
Check Caps Lock and letter case, try without spaces, and confirm the date format (for example DDMMYYYY vs DDMMYY). Some banks use a different format for older statements.

**Is it legal to remove a PDF password?**
It is fine for your own documents or files you are authorised to use. Don’t use it to bypass restrictions on documents that belong to someone else.

## Compress PDF

Compress PDF files in your browser: shrink scans and image-heavy PDFs, or hit a target like 200 KB. Text stays sharp and selectable — free, private, no upload.

### At a glance

- Recompresses the images inside a PDF at **72, 150 or 220 DPI**; text, fonts and vector graphics are never rasterised, so text stays sharp and selectable.
- **Target size** mode tries stronger settings step by step until the PDF fits a limit such as 100 KB, 200 KB or 1 MB.
- **Lossless** mode only optimises the file structure (object streams and maximum Flate compression with QPDF) and leaves image quality untouched.
- The result is checked to have the same number of pages, and if compression can’t make a file smaller, the original is returned.
- Compress several PDFs at once and download them individually or as a ZIP.
- PDFs that need a password to open must be unlocked first; permission-restricted PDFs are compressed with their restrictions kept (images re-encoded, not resized).
- Runs entirely in your browser: files are never uploaded.

### Features

- **Text stays text**: Many online compressors turn every page into a picture. Here only the images are optimised, so text remains searchable, selectable and razor-sharp.
- **Hit an exact size**: Need a PDF under 200 KB for an application portal? Enter the limit and the tool finds the best quality that fits.
- **Smart image resampling**: Photos and scans are resized to the resolution the page actually needs, then re-encoded — the biggest saving in most large PDFs.
- **Lossless option**: When quality must not change, lossless mode repacks the file’s structure with QPDF for a smaller file with identical content.
- **Never worse**: Every result is validated. If a PDF is already well optimised, you get the original back instead of a bigger or broken file.
- **Private and unlimited**: Contracts, statements and certificates stay on your device. No upload, no watermark, no file limit.

### How to compress a PDF

1. **Add your PDF** — Drop one or more PDF files onto the box, or click to browse.
2. **Choose how much to compress** — Pick **Recommended** for most files, **Extreme** for the smallest size, **Light** for print quality, or **Lossless** to keep images unchanged. Or switch to **Target file size** and enter a limit in KB.
3. **Check the result** — Each file shows its size before and after. Change the level and it recompresses automatically.
4. **Download** — Download each compressed PDF, or all of them as a ZIP.

### Guide

#### Which compression level should I use?

| Level | Images | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extreme | 72 DPI, strong compression | The smallest file for screen viewing and strict upload limits |
| Recommended | 150 DPI, good quality | Email, sharing and most upload portals |
| Light | 220 DPI, high quality | Documents that may be printed |
| Lossless | Unchanged | Files where image quality must not change |

#### Why some PDFs barely shrink

Most of a large PDF’s size is usually photos or scanned pages — that is what compression targets. A PDF that is mostly text and vector graphics, or one whose images are already small, has little to remove, and the tool will tell you it kept the original.

Embedded fonts, forms and attachments are left intact. Scanned documents saved as PNG-style images inside the PDF are converted to JPEG only in lossy modes and only where that makes them smaller.

#### Compressing PDFs for application portals

- Use **Target file size** and enter the portal’s limit — common limits are 100 KB, 200 KB, 500 KB and 1 MB.
- Scan documents in **greyscale at 150–200 DPI** rather than colour at 600 DPI; the PDF will be far smaller before you even compress it.
- Upload **one document per PDF**. If a portal needs several pages in one file, [merge them](https://justtools.online/pdf-tools/merge-pdf/) first, then compress.
- Check the compressed file is readable before submitting — zoom in on the smallest text.

### FAQ

**Is it safe to compress confidential PDFs here?**
Yes. Compression runs entirely in your browser, so the PDF is never uploaded or stored anywhere.

**Will compressing a PDF reduce quality?**
Only images are affected, and only in the lossy levels. Text, fonts and vector graphics stay exactly as they were. Choose **Light** or **Lossless** if image quality matters most.

**How do I compress a PDF to 200 KB (or 100 KB)?**
Switch to **Target file size**, choose 200 KB (or type any limit) and add your PDF. The tool steps down image resolution and quality until the file fits.

**Why didn’t my PDF get smaller?**
It was probably already optimised or made mostly of text. The tool never returns a larger file — you get the original back. For a text-only PDF, lossless mode may still save a little.

**Can I compress a password-protected PDF?**
If it needs a password to open, unlock it first with the [PDF Password Remover](https://justtools.online/pdf-tools/pdf-password-remover/) (you need to know the password), then compress the unlocked copy. PDFs that open freely but restrict printing or editing are compressed with their restrictions kept; their images are re-encoded but can only be resized after the restrictions are removed.

**Is the text still searchable after compression?**
Yes. Pages are not converted to images, so text stays selectable and searchable.

**How many PDFs can I compress at once?**
As many as you like. They are processed one after another and can be downloaded together as a ZIP.

**Does it work on mobile?**
Yes, in current mobile browsers. Very large PDFs (hundreds of MB) may need a computer with more memory.
