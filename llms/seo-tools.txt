## UTM Builder

Build UTM campaign links for Google Analytics 4: presets for WhatsApp, Instagram, email and ads, the GA4 channel each lands in, and a QR code for it.

### At a glance

- Adds **utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign**, and optionally **utm_id, utm_term** and **utm_content** to any page URL — keeping its other parameters and #fragment.
- One-click presets for **WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, LinkedIn, X, Google Ads, Meta ads, email, SMS, push notifications and QR codes**.
- Shows which **GA4 default channel** the link will most likely count as — Organic Social, Paid Search, Email and so on — using Google’s published rules.
- Keeps values **consistent**: lowercase and underscores by default, because GA4 treats “Facebook” and “facebook” as different sources.
- Warns about missing values, capital letters and short links, and reads the UTM tags back out of any link you paste.
- Builds a separate link for **several channels at once** (copy all or download CSV), and hands any link to the QR Code Generator for posters and packaging.

### Features

- **Know what works**: See in Google Analytics whether WhatsApp, Instagram or your newsletter brought the visits and sales.
- **GA4 channel preview**: Avoid “Unassigned” traffic: the tool shows the channel group before you share the link.
- **Consistent naming**: Automatic lowercase and space handling keep your reports clean, campaign after campaign.
- **Many channels, one click**: Tick the places you’ll post — get a separate tagged link for each, ready to copy or save as CSV.
- **QR codes for print**: Send the tagged link straight to the QR Code Generator to track posters, flyers and packaging.
- **Private**: Links are built in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server or shortened through a third party.

### How to build a UTM link

1. **Paste the page URL** — Enter the address of the page the link should open, such as your sale page.
2. **Pick a preset** — Click **WhatsApp**, **Instagram post**, **Google Ads** or another preset to fill in source and medium.
3. **Name the campaign** — Enter a campaign name like **diwali_sale_2026**, and optionally content such as **story_1**.
4. **Copy and share** — Copy the link, open it to test, or make a QR code. Check the GA4 channel shown below it.

### Guide

#### UTM parameters explained

| Parameter | Answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| utm_source | Where did the visit come from? | whatsapp, instagram, google, newsletter |
| utm_medium | What kind of channel? | social, cpc, email, sms, referral |
| utm_campaign | Which campaign or promotion? | diwali_sale_2026 |
| utm_content | Which link, ad or post exactly? | story_2, banner_top, button_red |
| utm_term | Which paid keyword? | running_shoes |
| utm_id | Which campaign ID (for cost import)? | fb_2026_q4 |

#### Tips for clean campaign data

**Stick to one naming style.** Decide on lowercase with underscores and use it everywhere — “Instagram”, “instagram” and “IG” become three separate sources in GA4. Use the medium names GA4 recognises (social, cpc, email, sms, referral, affiliate, display) so visits land in the right channel instead of “Unassigned”.

**Never tag internal links.** Links from one page of your site to another should not have UTM tags: clicking one starts a new session and overwrites where the visitor really came from. Google Ads links are tagged automatically (gclid) when auto-tagging is on, so they don’t need manual UTMs.

### FAQ

**What is a UTM link?**
A normal link with extra parameters, such as ?utm_source=whatsapp&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=diwali_sale, that tell Google Analytics where a visitor came from. The page itself works exactly the same.

**Which UTM parameters are required?**
utm_source is the essential one — Google Analytics ignores the others without it. Always add utm_medium and utm_campaign too, so the visit is grouped into a channel and campaign.

**What UTM should I use for WhatsApp?**
utm_source=whatsapp and utm_medium=social, which GA4 groups as Organic Social. Add utm_content to tell apart a status update, a broadcast list and a group message.

**Why does my traffic show as “Unassigned” in GA4?**
The source and medium didn’t match any of GA4’s default channel rules — for example a made-up medium like “post” or “story”. Use standard mediums such as social, email or cpc. The tool shows the channel before you share.

**Are UTM parameters case-sensitive?**
Yes. GA4 treats Facebook and facebook as two different sources, so keep “Lowercase” switched on.

**Do UTM tags affect SEO?**
Not when used on links in campaigns, ads, emails and social posts. Don’t use them on your own internal links, and make sure pages have a canonical URL so tagged links aren’t indexed separately.

#### How the score works

Every check has a weight by how much it affects search: the title, meta description and H1 count most, a missing X card least. A passed check earns its full weight, a warning half, a failure nothing; notes marked with an “i” are advice and don’t count. Each area gets its own score, so you can see at a glance whether the trouble is content, indexing or technical.

**If search engines can’t index the page at all** — an error status, a noindex tag or a robots.txt block — the score is capped at 30, because nothing else matters until that is fixed. A high score means the page is technically ready to rank; how well it ranks still depends on the quality of the content, links from other sites and the competition.

#### SEO for AI search (GEO)

ChatGPT search, Claude, Perplexity and Google’s AI Overviews answer questions by reading web pages and citing them. To be one of those sources, a page has to be **crawlable by their bots** (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, and Googlebot for AI Overviews), **quotable** (no nosnippet or tiny max-snippet), and **readable without JavaScript**, because most AI crawlers don’t run it.

Blocking training crawlers such as GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot or Google-Extended is a separate choice: it keeps your content out of model training but doesn’t remove you from search or from AI answers. The checker lists both kinds separately so you can decide. Clear headings, direct answers, FAQs and structured data help AI tools understand and quote a page; llms.txt is an optional extra that Google doesn’t use.

Yes — free, with no sign-up and no daily limit. The page-fetching service allows about 20 checks a minute from one network, which is plenty for normal use.

**How long should a title tag and meta description be?**
Keep titles to about 50–60 characters — Google cuts them at roughly 600 pixels — and meta descriptions to 120–160 characters. Bing Webmaster Tools reports descriptions shorter than about 25 or longer than 160 characters as an error.

**My page scores 100. Why doesn’t it rank?**
The checker covers on-page and technical SEO — what you control on the page. Rankings also depend on how useful the content is compared with competing pages, links from other websites, and real-user page speed. Use [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console) to see the searches you appear for.

**Should I block GPTBot and other AI crawlers?**
It depends on what you want. Blocking training crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Google-Extended) keeps your content out of AI training without hurting search. Blocking AI search crawlers (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot) means those assistants can’t cite or link to you.

**Why can’t the checker fetch my page?**
Some sites block automated requests with Cloudflare or similar bot protection, and pages behind a login or on localhost aren’t reachable from outside. Open the page, press Ctrl+U to view its source, copy everything and use **Paste HTML** instead.

**Does it check page speed and Core Web Vitals?**
It flags the common causes it can see in the HTML — render-blocking scripts, images without width and height, very large pages and missing lazy loading. For measured loading times use Google PageSpeed Insights.

**Does it work for single-page apps built with React, Vue or Angular?**
Yes. By default it checks the HTML your server sends, which is what most AI crawlers see; if the page is an empty app shell it says so. Switch on **Run JavaScript first** to check the page as it looks after rendering, the way Google sees it.

Free XML sitemap generator: crawl your site or paste URLs, skip noindex, redirected and robots.txt-blocked pages, add lastmod and images, ready for Google.

### At a glance

- **Crawls** a website from its home page, following links on the same host — up to 1,000 pages — or builds a sitemap from a **pasted list**, a CSV or an existing sitemap.
- Includes only **indexable pages**: it obeys robots.txt, and leaves out pages with noindex, a canonical pointing elsewhere, redirects and error statuses — listing each one with the reason.
- Adds **lastmod** from the page’s own date (article:modified_time, JSON-LD dateModified) or the server’s Last-Modified header, or a date you choose.
- Optionally adds **images** (Google image sitemap extension) and linked PDF files; changefreq and priority are optional because Google ignores them.
- Splits large sites into several files with a **sitemap index** at the protocol’s 50,000-URL / 50 MB limit, and also exports a plain-text list.
- Pages are fetched by your browser when the site allows it, otherwise through Jina Reader at about 18 pages a minute. Only page addresses are sent.

### Features

- **Crawls like Google**: Follows links, respects robots.txt and nofollow, and only keeps pages that can actually be indexed.
- **Clean by default**: No duplicates, no redirects, no noindex pages and no tracking parameters — the mistakes Search Console reports.
- **Honest lastmod**: Dates come from the pages themselves, so Google can trust them to spot updates.
- **Image sitemaps**: Add each page’s images to help them appear in Google Images.
- **Any size**: Sitemap index and numbered files are made automatically for very large sites.
- **From a list too**: Paste URLs or open a CSV or old sitemap to clean it up and rebuild it.

### How to create an XML sitemap

1. **Enter your website** — Type your home page address and choose how many pages to crawl, or switch to **From a list of URLs**.
2. **Let it crawl** — Pages are discovered through links; skipped pages are listed with the reason. You can stop at any time.
3. **Choose the details** — Pick where **lastmod** comes from and whether to add images; leave changefreq and priority out unless you need them.
4. **Upload and submit** — Download **sitemap.xml**, upload it to your site’s root, add it to robots.txt and submit it in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.

### Guide

#### What belongs in a sitemap

| Include | Leave out |
| --- | --- |
| Pages that return 200 and that you want in search results | Pages with noindex, or blocked in robots.txt |
| The canonical version of each page | Duplicates, ?ref= and other tracking variants |
| The final address after redirects | URLs that redirect or return 404 |
| Accurate lastmod dates | Dates that change on every build without a real update |

#### Do I need a sitemap?

Search engines find most pages by following links, but a sitemap helps them discover new and updated pages faster — especially on new sites with few backlinks, large sites, and pages that few other pages link to. Google reads **loc** and an accurate **lastmod**; it ignores changefreq and priority.

After uploading, list the sitemap in robots.txt (Sitemap: https://example.com/sitemap.xml) so every search engine finds it, and submit it once in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools to see how many pages are indexed. Google no longer accepts sitemap “pings”; Bing and Yandex also support IndexNow for instant updates.

### FAQ

**Is the sitemap generator free?**
Yes, with no sign-up. Crawls are limited to 1,000 pages at a time; for bigger sites, crawl sections separately or use the URL list mode with an export from your CMS.

**Why is the crawl slow for my site?**
If your site doesn’t allow other websites to read its pages (most don’t), pages are fetched through Jina Reader, whose free service allows about 20 requests a minute — so 100 pages take around 6 minutes. Sites that allow it are crawled several pages at a time.

**Why were some pages left out?**
They can’t be indexed as they are: blocked by robots.txt, marked noindex, redirected, returning an error, or pointing to a different canonical URL. The “Pages left out” list shows the reason for each.

**Should I set changefreq and priority?**
Not for Google — it ignores both. They are optional and left out by default; an accurate lastmod is far more useful.

**How many URLs can one sitemap hold?**
50,000 URLs or 50 MB uncompressed. Larger sites need several sitemap files listed in a sitemap index, which the generator creates automatically.

### How to create a robots.txt file

1. **Pick a starting point** — Choose a preset such as **WordPress** or **Block AI training**, or start from **Allow everything**.
2. **Add your rules** — List paths to keep out of search (like /admin/), allow or block specific crawlers, and add your sitemap URL.
3. **Test it** — Click **Test a URL against this file** to check important pages are still crawlable.
4. **Upload** — Download robots.txt and upload it to your site’s root so it opens at example.com/robots.txt.

### Guide

#### robots.txt rules at a glance

| Line | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User-agent | Which crawler the following rules are for; * means all | User-agent: GPTBot |
| Disallow | Paths the crawler must not fetch | Disallow: /admin/ |
| Allow | An exception inside a disallowed path | Allow: /admin/help/ |
| * | Matches any characters | Disallow: /*?sort= |
| $ | Marks the end of the URL | Disallow: /*.pdf$ |
| Sitemap | Full URL of a sitemap, anywhere in the file | Sitemap: https://example.com/sitemap.xml |

#### What robots.txt can and can’t do

robots.txt controls **crawling**, not indexing. A blocked page can still appear in Google as a bare link if other sites link to it, and Google can’t see a noindex tag on a page it isn’t allowed to fetch. To keep a page out of search results, allow crawling and add **<meta name="robots" content="noindex">** instead.

It is also public and voluntary: well-behaved crawlers such as Googlebot, Bingbot, GPTBot and ClaudeBot obey it, but it is not access control. Never list secret paths in it, and protect private areas with a login.

### FAQ

**Where do I put robots.txt?**
In the root of your domain, so it opens at https://example.com/robots.txt. Each subdomain (blog.example.com) needs its own file, and it must be plain text returning HTTP 200.

**How do I block ChatGPT and other AI bots?**
Add a group for each crawler with Disallow: /. To stop training only, block GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, CCBot and similar; to also leave AI search answers, block OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot. The “Block AI training” and “Block all AI” presets do this for you.

**Will blocking Google-Extended remove my site from Google Search?**
No. Google-Extended only controls whether Gemini may use your content for training and grounding. Search, including AI Overviews, uses Googlebot.

**Does Google follow Crawl-delay?**
No. Bing and Yandex do; Google adjusts its crawl rate automatically. If Googlebot overloads your server, return 503 or 429 temporarily.

**Why does the validator say noindex isn’t supported?**
Google stopped honouring Noindex lines in robots.txt in September 2019. Use a robots meta tag or the X-Robots-Tag HTTP header on the page instead.

**Is blocking CSS and JavaScript a problem?**
Yes. Google renders pages like a browser; without your CSS and JavaScript it may see a broken page and rank it lower. Keep folders such as /wp-includes/ and /_next/ crawlable.
