Which paper for what

  • Dot grid – bullet journals, lettering and sketches where full lines would be too busy. 5 mm is the usual journal spacing.
  • Isometric – drawings of 3D shapes, cubes and buildings: the lines run at 30° to the horizontal, so the three edges of a cube can be drawn along them.
  • Hexagon – board-game and fantasy maps, organic chemistry rings, quilting and tiling patterns.
  • Polar – plotting angles and directions, radar charts and polar equations, with rings for distance and rays every 5–30°.
  • Semi-log – quantities that grow or decay exponentially (bacteria, interest, radioactive decay) plot as straight lines.
  • Log-log – power laws such as y = x² or Kepler’s third law plot as straight lines.
  • Coordinate plane – plotting points, lines and shapes in algebra and geometry classes.

Printing at true size

Printers can scale a page without asking: “Fit to page” or “Shrink to printable area” makes every square a few percent too small. The PDF asks PDF readers to print at actual size, but check the print dialog says 100% or Actual size, then measure ten squares with a ruler.

Most printers cannot print in the outer 3–5 mm of the sheet, so keep a margin of at least that – the default is 10 mm.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I print graph paper at the right size?

Download the PDF and print it with the scale at 100% or Actual size – not “Fit to page”. The PDF is drawn in exact millimetres and asks the printer not to scale it. Measure ten squares to check: ten 5 mm squares should be 5 cm.

Is the graph paper free to use in class?

Yes. Print as many copies as you need for home, school or work. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no limit.

What size squares does normal graph paper have?

It depends on where you are: 5 mm squares are common in Europe and India, 1/4 inch squares (four per inch) in the United States, and 1 mm millimetre paper with heavier lines every centimetre for technical work. All are presets here, or type any size.

What is isometric graph paper?

Paper with lines at 30° to the horizontal (and usually upright lines too), forming equilateral triangles. It makes it easy to draw 3D objects such as cubes, because their edges follow the grid. Choose Dots for isometric dot paper.

What is semi-log paper used for?

One axis is logarithmic and the other is ordinary. Anything that grows or shrinks by the same factor in equal times – population, compound interest, radioactive decay – plots as a straight line on it. Log-log paper makes power laws straight.

Can I print a coordinate plane worksheet?

Yes. Choose Coordinate plane, pick one to six planes per page, all four quadrants or the first, and how far the axes run. Turn on Name & date for a worksheet heading.

Can I change the line colour?

Choose blue, green, grey, orange-red or black, or Custom for any colour for the lines and the heavier lines. Light colours are easier on the eyes and stay in the background of what you draw.
