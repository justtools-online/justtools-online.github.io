Interactive Periodic Table

Click any of the 118 elements for its properties, electron configuration and Bohr model; colour the table by electronegativity, radius or state of matter; then test yourself or print it.

Runs in your browser Free · no sign-up Works on any device

At a glance

  • Shows all 118 elements with IUPAC names and the IUPAC (CIAAW 2021) atomic weights; square brackets mark the mass number of the longest-lived isotope for the 34 elements with no stable one.
  • Each element’s page gives its electron configuration (checked against NIST for every element up to hassium), electrons per shell, a Bohr model, valence electrons, oxidation states, electronegativity, atomic radius, ionisation energy, electron affinity, melting and boiling points and density.
  • Colour the table 12 ways: by family, by block (s, p, d, f), metals / metalloids / nonmetals, state of matter, or as a heat map of any of 8 properties.
  • The state-of-matter view takes any temperature from 0 to 6,000 K and shows which elements are solid, liquid or gas at normal pressure — gallium and caesium melt at 37 °C; at 25 °C only bromine and mercury are liquids.
  • Physical data come from PubChem (US National Library of Medicine), which takes them from Jefferson Lab and Los Alamos National Laboratory; the page flags values that are estimates.
  • A quiz with three question types — find it, name it, give the symbol — for the first 20, first 30 or all 118 elements, with best scores kept on your device.
  • Printable periodic table as a vector PDF, PNG or SVG on A4, Letter, A3 or Tabloid, in colour or black and white — including blank and numbers-only practice sheets.
  • Free, no sign-up, and it works offline once loaded; nothing you do is sent anywhere.

Step by step

How to use the periodic table

  1. 1

    Find an element

    Click it in the table, or type its name, symbol or atomic number in the search box and press Enter.

  2. 2

    Read its properties

    The card under the table lists its configuration, shells, oxidation states, electronegativity, radius, energies, melting and boiling points and density. Use the arrows to step to the next element.

  3. 3

    Colour the table

    Pick Colour by — family, block, metals and nonmetals, state of matter, or a property heat map. Point at a legend entry to light up that group.

  4. 4

    Change the temperature

    In the state-of-matter view, drag the slider or pick a preset such as body temperature to see what melts and boils.

  5. 5

    Quiz yourself or print

    Choose Quiz to practise, or Print & download for a PDF, PNG or SVG of the table.

How the periodic table is arranged

Elements are placed in order of atomic number — the number of protons — in 7 rows called periods and 18 columns called groups. Elements in the same group have the same arrangement of outer electrons, which is why they behave alike: the alkali metals in group 1 all react with water, the halogens in group 17 all form salts, the noble gases in group 18 hardly react at all.

The table splits into blocks named after the subshell being filled: the s-block (groups 1–2 and helium), the p-block (groups 13–18), the d-block (groups 3–12, the transition metals) and the f-block (the two rows shown underneath, which would make the table 32 columns wide if fitted in).

This table puts lutetium and lawrencium in group 3, under scandium and yttrium, and the 14 elements from lanthanum to ytterbium and from actinium to nobelium in the f-block rows — the layout an IUPAC project report favoured in 2021. Many textbooks, including NCERT’s, put lanthanum and actinium in group 3 instead; the elements and their properties are the same either way.

Periodic trends at a glance

Trends have exceptions — nitrogen’s ionisation energy is higher than oxygen’s, and fluorine’s electron affinity is lower than chlorine’s — so the heat maps and the trend chart are worth a look rather than relying on the rule alone.

Where the data come from

  • Names and atomic weights: IUPAC and its Commission on Isotopic Abundances and Atomic Weights (CIAAW, 2021 abridged values).
  • Electron configurations: built by the same engine as our Electron Configuration Builder, which is checked against the NIST Atomic Spectra Database.
  • Electronegativity, radius, ionisation energy, electron affinity, oxidation states, melting and boiling points, density and families: PubChem’s periodic table (US National Library of Medicine), compiled from Jefferson Lab and Los Alamos National Laboratory. Three values are corrected: helium has no melting point at normal pressure, and carbon and arsenic sublime instead of melting.
  • Astatine, francium and the elements from fermium (100) on have never been made in a visible amount, so their physical values are estimates; from meitnerium (109) on, even the chemistry is a prediction. The page says so wherever it shows them.

The first 20 elements

Many school syllabuses ask for the first 20 (or 30) elements by heart. In order: hydrogen (H), helium (He), lithium (Li), beryllium (Be), boron (B), carbon (C), nitrogen (N), oxygen (O), fluorine (F), neon (Ne), sodium (Na), magnesium (Mg), aluminium (Al), silicon (Si), phosphorus (P), sulfur (S), chlorine (Cl), argon (Ar), potassium (K) and calcium (Ca). Use the quiz with First 20 to practise them until they stick.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How many elements are in the periodic table?

118. The last four — nihonium (113), moscovium (115), tennessine (117) and oganesson (118) — were named in 2016, completing the seventh period.

Why are some atomic weights in square brackets?

Those elements have no stable isotope, so they have no standard atomic weight. The number in brackets is the mass number of the longest-lived isotope, e.g. [97] for technetium.

Which elements are liquid at room temperature?

At 25 °C only bromine and mercury. Gallium (29.8 °C), caesium (28.4 °C) and rubidium (39.3 °C) melt just above it — set the state-of-matter slider to body temperature and gallium and caesium turn liquid.

Where do the lanthanides and actinides go?

In the two rows under the main table. They belong between groups 2 and 3 but would make the table 32 columns wide, so they are printed separately. This table puts lutetium and lawrencium in group 3; some books put lanthanum and actinium there.

What do the colours mean?

In the default view each colour is a family: alkali metals, alkaline earth metals, transition metals, post-transition metals, metalloids, nonmetals, halogens, noble gases, lanthanides and actinides. Point at a legend entry to light up its members. Other views colour by block, by metal type, by state or by the size of a property.

Can I print the periodic table?

Yes. Choose Print & download, pick the paper (A4, Letter, A3 or Tabloid), colour or black and white, and what each box shows — full details, symbols, numbers only or blank. Print it directly, or download a PDF, PNG or SVG. Keep the scale at 100% when printing the PDF.

Is the electron configuration shown measured or predicted?

For elements up to hassium (108) it matches NIST’s measured ground states, including exceptions such as chromium ([Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹) and copper ([Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹). Beyond that the configurations come from theory.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Everything — data, quiz and PDF — runs in your browser. Settings and quiz best scores are saved on your device only.
