Solitaire

The classic card game, done properly — draw one or three, Windows or Vegas scoring, deals you can always win, and the bouncing cards when you do.

At a glance

  • Classic Klondike rules: build the seven columns down in alternating colours, and the four foundations up by suit from ace to king. Choose Draw 1 (easier) or Draw 3 (the classic challenge).
  • Winnable deals only: before you see a deal, a built-in solver checks it can be won. Even knowing where every card is, only about 82% of draw-three deals can be won (Blake and Gent, 2019).
  • Windows scoring (10 for a card on a foundation, 5 for turning a card, a time bonus of 700,000 ÷ seconds), Vegas scoring ($52 for the deck, $5 a card back), or no score at all.
  • Tap a card to send it where it fits, or drag it. Safe cards go up to the foundations by themselves, and Finish plays out the rest once every card is face up.
  • Unlimited undo, hints, a daily deal, deals you can share by number, and a Solve it for me button that plays out a winning line.
  • Stats for every mode — win rate, streaks, fastest win and best score — and your game in progress are saved on this device. Free, no sign-up.

Step by step

How to play solitaire

  1. 1

    Aim for the foundations

    Move all 52 cards to the four foundations at the top, one suit each, from ace up to king.

  2. 2

    Build down in the columns

    In the seven columns, put a card on one rank higher of the opposite colour — a red 6 on a black 7. You can move a whole run of cards together.

  3. 3

    Turn up hidden cards

    When you move the face-up cards off a column, the card below turns over. Only a king (or a run starting with one) can fill an empty column.

  4. 4

    Draw from the stock

    Tap the stock to turn one or three cards onto the waste. The top waste card can be played. When the stock runs out, tap again to turn the waste over.

  5. 5

    Win

    Once every card is face up, press Finish and the rest go up by themselves.

Solitaire tips

  • Turn up hidden cards first. Moves that reveal a face-down card are almost always the best — especially in the columns with the most hidden cards.
  • Don’t empty a column without a king to fill it. An empty column only takes a king, so clear one when a king is waiting.
  • Think before playing from the waste. It’s often better to free cards in the columns first and use the stock later.
  • Keep the colours balanced. If you build a long red-black run, make sure the other colour of the next card is still reachable.
  • Don’t rush cards up. A card on a foundation can’t hold the next lower card; keep useful cards in play until they are safe to move up.

About Klondike

Klondike is the best-known patience game, so well known in the US and Canada that “solitaire” on its own usually means Klondike; in the UK it is often just called patience. Where its name comes from is unknown — the late-1890s Klondike Gold Rush is a popular guess, but nothing proves it.

Its fame today owes a lot to Microsoft, which included Solitaire with Windows 3.0 in 1990, partly to teach people to use a mouse by dragging cards. Whether a random deal can be won is still an open question in mathematics; with every card known, about 82% of draw-three deals can be won, but nobody has worked out the exact figure for the real game, where most cards are hidden.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is every solitaire game winnable?

No. Some deals can’t be won however well you play — even knowing where every card is, roughly one draw-three deal in five is impossible. With Winnable deals only on, the solver checks each deal before you play it, so every game here can be won.

What is the difference between draw 1 and draw 3?

In Draw 1 you turn one card from the stock at a time, so you can reach every card — it’s easier. In Draw 3 you turn three at a time and can only play the top one, so many cards stay buried; this is the classic, harder game.

How does Vegas scoring work?

You “pay” $52 for the deck and win $5 for each card you move to the foundations, so you need 11 cards to break even. You get one pass through the stock drawing one card, or three passes drawing three. Your running total is kept.

How is the score calculated?

Standard scoring follows Windows Solitaire: 10 points for a card to a foundation, 5 for playing from the waste to a column or turning over a card, −15 for moving a card back down, and −100 for turning the waste over when drawing one. Time takes 2 points every 10 seconds, and a win adds 700,000 ÷ your seconds.

Can I play the same deal again or share it?

Yes. Restart replays the current deal, and Share this deal gives a link with the game number, so a friend gets exactly the same cards.

Does it save my game?

Yes — your game in progress, settings and stats are saved in this browser. Close the tab and carry on later.

Is it free?

Yes, completely free with no download and no sign-up. It works on phones, tablets and computers.
