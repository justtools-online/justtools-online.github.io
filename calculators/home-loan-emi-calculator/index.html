Home Loan EMI Calculator

Plan your home loan in one place: the EMI and full schedule, how much prepayments and rate changes matter, the most you can borrow on a property, the tax you save and the loan your income supports.

Runs in your browser Free · no sign-up Works on any device

At a glance

  • Example: a ₹50 lakh loan at 8.5% for 20 years has an EMI of ₹43,391 and total interest of ₹54,13,879 – more than the loan itself.
  • Prepayments: an extra ₹1 lakh once a year on that loan saves ₹18.55 lakh of interest and closes it 6 years sooner; ₹5,000 more every month saves ₹13.89 lakh.
  • Choose whether a prepayment shortens the tenure or lowers the EMI, and add one-time prepayments such as a bonus.
  • Floating rates: a rise from 8.5% to 9.5% after 3 years keeps the EMI but adds 3 years 3 months and ₹16.9 lakh of interest – or raises the EMI to ₹46,265 to finish on time.
  • Checks RBI’s loan-to-value limits – 90% for loans up to ₹30 lakh, 80% up to ₹75 lakh, 75% above – and shows the largest loan the property allows.
  • Tax benefit (old regime): interest up to ₹2 lakh and principal within ₹1.5 lakh of 80C a year, per co-owner borrower – ₹93,448 saved in year 1 at the 30% slab. The new regime gives none for a self-occupied home.
  • Eligibility from income and other EMIs, a year-by-year schedule with prepayments and a CSV download. Nothing you enter leaves your browser.

Reduce the EMI or the tenure?

When you prepay, lenders usually let you keep the EMI and finish sooner, or keep the end date and pay a smaller EMI. Shortening the tenure saves far more interest, because the whole EMI keeps working against a smaller balance. Lowering the EMI makes sense when you need breathing space in the monthly budget.

Under RBI’s pre-payment directions, floating-rate loans to individuals for non-business purposes sanctioned or renewed from 1 January 2026 cannot carry pre-payment charges, whatever the source of the money.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How is home loan EMI calculated?

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan, r the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n the number of months. ₹50 lakh at 8.5% for 240 months gives ₹43,391.

Is it better to prepay or invest?

Prepaying saves the loan’s interest rate with certainty, after tax. If you are in the old regime and still within the ₹2 lakh interest deduction, the saving is a little lower. Many borrowers do both – keep an emergency fund and investments going, and prepay windfalls such as bonuses.

Should I reduce my EMI or tenure after prepaying?

Reducing the tenure saves much more interest; reducing the EMI eases your monthly budget. Switch between Shorten the tenure and Lower the EMI in the calculator to see the difference for your loan.

How much home loan can I get on my salary?

Lenders typically let all your EMIs together take up 40–60% of your take-home pay. With ₹1 lakh a month and no other EMIs, 50% allows an EMI of ₹50,000 – about ₹57.6 lakh at 8.5% for 20 years. The property’s loan-to-value limit can cap it further.

Can I claim home loan benefits in the new tax regime?

Not for a home you live in: the new regime allows neither the interest deduction nor 80C for principal. For a let-out home, interest can still be set off against that home’s rental income.

What happens when the repo rate changes?

Repo-linked loans reset at intervals set in your agreement. Lenders usually keep the EMI and change the tenure; you can ask to change the EMI instead. Use See a rate change to compare both.
