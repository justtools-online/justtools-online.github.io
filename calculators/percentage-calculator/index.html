Percentage change is not symmetric

Going from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase, but going back from 100 to 80 is a 20% decrease — the change is always measured against the starting value. That is also why a 50% loss needs a 100% gain to recover. When neither number is the “start”, use the percentage difference, which measures the gap against their average.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by the number. 15% of 200 is 15 ÷ 100 × 200 = 30.

How do I calculate my marks percentage?

Divide the marks you got by the total marks and multiply by 100. For 450 out of 500: 450 ÷ 500 × 100 = 90%. Use the Marks percentage card to add several subjects.

How do I calculate percentage increase?

Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. From 80 to 100: (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%.

How do I find the original price before GST or a discount?

Divide by (1 + rate ÷ 100) for an increase such as GST, or by (1 − rate ÷ 100) for a discount. ₹118 including 18% GST was ₹118 ÷ 1.18 = ₹100. Use the Original value before a % change card.

What is the difference between percentage change and percentage difference?

Percentage change compares a new value with an old one. Percentage difference compares two values with no “before” and “after”, dividing the gap by their average.

Is anything I type stored?

No. All calculations happen in your browser.
