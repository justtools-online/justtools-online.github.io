Car Loan EMI Calculator

Work out the EMI on your new or used car from the on-road price, see what a dealer’s flat rate really costs, compare 3 to 7 year loans and check what closing the loan early saves.

Runs in your browser Free · no sign-up Works on any device

At a glance

  • Starts from the on-road price: ex-showroom price + RTO and road tax + insurance + accessories, less your down payment.
  • Example: a ₹9.2 lakh loan at 9% for 5 years has an EMI of ₹19,098 and total interest of ₹2,25,861.
  • Converts a flat rate to the reducing-balance rate it really is: 7% flat for 5 years costs 12.50% a year, 8% flat costs 14.13%.
  • Adds the processing fee with 18% GST to the cost and shows the true yearly cost (APR) – a 0.5% fee lifts 9% to about 9.25%.
  • Compares 3- to 7-year loans side by side: at 9%, stretching ₹9.2 lakh from 3 to 7 years halves the EMI but raises the interest from ₹1.33 lakh to ₹3.23 lakh.
  • Early closure: the outstanding amount, the foreclosure charge with GST and what you save. RBI rules bar pre-payment charges on floating-rate loans to individuals sanctioned or renewed from 1 January 2026.
  • Checks the 20/4/10 rule of thumb and builds a full year-by-year and month-by-month schedule. Nothing you enter leaves your browser.

Car loan EMI formula

Banks work out a car loan EMI on the reducing balance: EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan, r the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n the number of months. Each EMI pays the month’s interest on what you still owe, and the rest reduces the loan.

With a flat rate, interest is worked out on the full loan for the whole term – P × rate × years – and added to the loan before dividing by the months. Because you are paying interest on money you have already repaid, a flat rate costs much more than the same number on a reducing balance.

The 20/4/10 rule

A popular rule of thumb for buying a car: put down at least 20%, borrow for no more than 4 years, and keep the EMI – ideally all car costs, including fuel and insurance – within 10% of your income. It is a guide rather than a rule, but it keeps a car affordable for the whole loan.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How is car loan EMI calculated?

With the reducing-balance formula EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r the monthly interest rate and n the number of months. For ₹9.2 lakh at 9% a year for 60 months, r = 0.0075 and the EMI is ₹19,098.

Is a flat interest rate cheaper?

No – it only looks cheaper. A flat rate charges interest on the original loan for the whole term even as you repay it. A 7% flat rate over 5 years costs the same as 12.5% a year on a reducing balance. Choose Flat in the calculator to see the equivalent rate before you compare offers.

Is the loan on the ex-showroom or the on-road price?

Lenders fund a share of the car’s price – some of the ex-showroom price, others of the on-road price – and you pay the rest as a down payment. This calculator starts from the on-road price, the total you actually pay, so the down payment covers whatever the loan does not.

Can I close my car loan early?

Usually yes, after a minimum number of EMIs set by your lender, sometimes with a foreclosure charge on the outstanding amount plus GST. Car loans are often fixed-rate, so check your sanction letter. Under RBI’s pre-payment directions, floating-rate loans to individuals for non-business purposes sanctioned or renewed from 1 January 2026 carry no pre-payment charge.

What is a good tenure for a car loan?

Shorter is cheaper: the table shows that the same loan costs about 2.4 times as much interest over 7 years as over 3. Many people pick the shortest tenure whose EMI fits comfortably in their budget – often 4 or 5 years.

Does the processing fee matter?

Yes. A fee paid upfront means you borrow less than you repay on, which raises the true cost. The calculator adds 18% GST to the fee and shows the APR, so a lower rate with a high fee can be compared fairly with a higher rate and no fee.
