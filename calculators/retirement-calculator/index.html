Retirement Calculator

How much you need to retire, whether you’re on track, and what to invest each month if you’re not.

Runs in your browser Free · no sign-up Works on any device

At a glance

  • Works out the corpus you need at retirement to pay expenses that rise with inflation every year, until the age you plan for, while the rest keeps earning.
  • Projects your current savings and a step-up SIP (raised every year) to retirement, month by month.
  • Shows the shortfall or surplus, the extra monthly SIP — or lump sum today — that puts you on track.
  • Tells you until what age your money lasts on the current plan.
  • A chart of your money by age — growing while you work, then paying your expenses — with the on-track plan beside it, and a table view.
  • Separate returns before and after retirement, since most people move to safer investments once they retire.

An example

A 30-year-old who spends ₹50,000 a month today and wants to retire at 60 will spend about ₹2.87 lakh a month at 60 with 6% inflation. To pay that — rising every year — until 85, while earning 7% in retirement, they need about ₹7.48 crore at 60. With ₹5 lakh saved and a ₹10,000 SIP raised 5% a year at 12%, they’d have about ₹6.17 crore: ₹1.31 crore short, and the money would run out around 80. Investing about ₹2,800 more a month (also raised 5% a year) closes the gap.

Rules of thumb

RuleWhat it says
25–30 × yearly expensesA corpus of about 25 to 30 times your first year’s expenses lasts a long retirement in India, where inflation is higher.
Start earlyEach year of delay needs a noticeably bigger SIP, because the money has less time to compound.
Step up your SIPRaising the SIP with your salary every year reaches the goal with a far smaller starting amount.
Keep health apartMedical costs rise faster than inflation — hold health insurance and an emergency fund separately.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to retire in India?

It depends on your expenses, inflation and how long you plan for. A common guide is 25–30 times your yearly expenses at retirement; this calculator works it out exactly for your numbers.

What inflation should I assume?

India’s consumer inflation has averaged around 5–6% a year; 6% is a reasonable planning figure. A higher figure gives a safer plan.

What return should I expect?

Diversified equity funds have returned around 10–12% a year over long periods, but with ups and downs. After retiring, a safer mix may earn 6–8%. Use conservative figures.

Should I include EPF and PPF?

Yes — add your current EPF, PPF and NPS balances to savings so far, and count your monthly contributions in the SIP.

What is a step-up SIP?

A SIP you raise every year, for example by 5–10% as your salary grows. It makes reaching a large corpus much easier.

What if I retire early?

Set the retirement age lower and plan until 90 or more. You’ll have fewer years to save and more years to fund, so the corpus needed rises sharply.
