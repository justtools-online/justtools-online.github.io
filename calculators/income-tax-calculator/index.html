Income Tax Calculator

Work out your income tax for FY 2026-27 or 2025-26 under both regimes – salary, HRA, house property, interest and deductions – and see which regime saves more, with every step of the calculation.

Runs in your browser Free · no sign-up Works on any device

At a glance

  • New-regime slabs for FY 2026-27, unchanged by Budget 2026: nil up to ₹4 lakh, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20% and 25% in steps of ₹4 lakh, and 30% above ₹24 lakh.
  • No tax up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income in the new regime thanks to the rebate (section 87A, section 156 of the 2025 Act) – a salary of ₹12.75 lakh after the ₹75,000 standard deduction – with marginal relief just above.
  • Old regime: nil up to ₹2.5 lakh (₹3 lakh at 60–79, ₹5 lakh at 80+), then 5%, 20% and 30%, with a rebate up to ₹5 lakh and a ₹50,000 standard deduction.
  • HRA is the least of the HRA received, rent minus 10% of basic, and 50% of basic in metro cities (40% elsewhere). From FY 2026-27, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad join Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai at 50%.
  • Old-regime deductions with their limits: 80C ₹1.5 lakh, NPS 80CCD(1B) ₹50,000, 80D, 80E, 80G, 80TTA/80TTB and home-loan interest up to ₹2 lakh. The new regime allows the employer’s NPS contribution up to 14% of basic.
  • Surcharge of 10%, 15% and 25% (37% in the old regime above ₹5 crore) with marginal relief, and 4% health and education cess.
  • Shows which regime is cheaper, by how much, and the extra deductions the old regime would need to break even. Everything stays in your browser.

What changed in 2026

The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. It speaks of a single “tax year” instead of the previous year and assessment year, and renumbers the sections – the 87A rebate, for example, is now section 156 – but Budget 2026 kept the rates, the ₹12 lakh rebate limit and the ₹75,000 standard deduction.

Under the new Income-tax Rules, 2026, HRA in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad is worked out at 50% of salary, like Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai. For FY 2025-26 those four cities stay at 40%.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free?

In the new regime, yes: if your taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less, the rebate wipes out the tax. For salaried people, the ₹75,000 standard deduction makes a salary of up to ₹12.75 lakh tax-free. The rebate does not apply to income taxed at special rates, such as capital gains on shares.

Which tax regime is better for me?

It depends on your deductions. The new regime has lower rates but few deductions; the old one rewards large claims such as HRA, 80C, 80D and home-loan interest. Enter your figures and the calculator shows both totals, the cheaper regime and how much more you would need to claim for the old one to win.

How is HRA exemption calculated?

It is the least of three amounts: the HRA you receive; the rent you pay minus 10% of basic pay and DA; and 50% of basic and DA in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai – plus Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad from 2026-27 – or 40% elsewhere. It is available only in the old regime.

Can I switch between the old and new regimes?

If you have no business income, you can choose either regime every year when you file your return. Tell your employer your choice so that TDS is deducted correctly; the new regime applies if you do not.

What is marginal relief?

It stops a small rise in income from causing a bigger rise in tax. Just above ₹12 lakh in the new regime, your tax cannot be more than the income above ₹12 lakh. The same idea applies at the surcharge thresholds of ₹50 lakh, ₹1 crore, ₹2 crore and ₹5 crore.

Does this calculator include capital gains?

No. It covers salary, pension, house property, interest and other income taxed at slab rates for resident individuals. Gains on shares and mutual funds are taxed at special rates with their own rules, so add them separately or ask a tax professional.
