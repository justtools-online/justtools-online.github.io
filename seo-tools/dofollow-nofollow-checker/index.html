Dofollow Nofollow Link Checker

Enter a page address to list every link on it and see which ones search engines are asked to follow. Check whether a page links to your site — and whether that backlink is followed, nofollow, sponsored or UGC.

Fetches the page you enter Free · no sign-up Works on any device

At a glance

  • Lists every link on a page and labels each as followed (dofollow), nofollow, sponsored or UGC, with its anchor text, address and rel values.
  • Counts followed and nofollow links, internal and external links, different addresses and the outside sites linked to.
  • Checks a backlink: type your domain to see each link the page gives you and whether it is followed.
  • Reads the page’s robots meta tag: <meta name="robots" content="nofollow"> makes every link on the page nofollow, and the tool counts them so.
  • Treats sponsored and ugc like nofollow, as Google does — links with any of the three “will generally not be followed”.
  • Warns about nofollow on your own internal links, links with no anchor text, and the meaningless rel="dofollow".
  • Works from a page address or from pasted HTML — useful for pages behind a login. Pasted HTML never leaves your device.
  • Filter, search, copy the addresses or download the list as CSV.

What the rel values mean

In the linkShown asMeaning
no rel, or only noopener, noreferrer, externalFollowedAn ordinary link. Search engines may follow it and count it as a vote for the page it points to.
rel="nofollow"NofollowThe site does not want to be associated with the linked page, or have it crawled from here.
rel="sponsored"SponsoredAn advertisement or paid placement. Google’s preferred value for paid links.
rel="ugc"UGCUser-generated content, such as comments and forum posts.
rel="ugc nofollow"Nofollow + UGCValues can be combined, separated by spaces or commas.
<meta name="robots" content="nofollow"> in the pageNofollow (page)Every link on the page is nofollow, whatever its own rel.

There is no “dofollow” attribute

“Dofollow” is only a name for a normal link — one without nofollow, sponsored or ugc. Links are followed by default, so nothing needs to be added. Writing rel="dofollow" does nothing; if a page uses it, the tool says so.

Since 2019 Google has treated nofollow, sponsored and ugc as hints rather than strict orders: such links will generally not be followed, but Google may choose to use them. Other search engines make their own choices.

What counts as a link here

  • Links to web pages — <a href> and image-map <area href> — are counted and labelled.
  • Jumps within the same page (#section), e-mail and phone links and javascript: links do not lead to another page. They are listed under Not pages and left out of the counts.
  • A link to the same site with or without www. is internal. Links to other subdomains, such as blog.example.com from example.com, count as external.
  • Links inside <noscript> and <template> are ignored, as they are not part of the page a visitor sees.

What it cannot see

  • Links added by JavaScript after the page loads, unless Run JavaScript first is on.
  • A nofollow sent as an X-Robots-Tag HTTP header. Browsers do not let one website read another’s headers.
  • Pages behind a login, or sites that block automated visitors. Open the page yourself, copy its source (Ctrl+U) and use Paste HTML.
  • Whether a search engine really followed a link. The tool reports what the page asks for.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between dofollow and nofollow links?

A dofollow link is a normal link: search engines can follow it and treat it as a recommendation of the page it points to. A nofollow link carries rel="nofollow" (or sponsored or ugc), which asks search engines not to follow it or pass ranking credit through it.

How do I check if a backlink is dofollow?

Enter the address of the page that links to you and click Check links. Then type your domain in the backlink box. The tool lists each link to your site and labels it Followed, Nofollow, Sponsored or UGC.

Are sponsored and UGC links nofollow?

In effect, yes. Google introduced sponsored for paid links and ugc for user-generated content in 2019, and says links marked with any of the three values will generally not be followed. The tool counts all three as nofollow and shows which value each link uses.

Why does the tool say all links are nofollow when they have no rel?

The page has a robots meta tag with nofollow (or none). That applies to every link on the page at once. The tool shows the tag and marks such links “Nofollow (page)”.

Should my internal links be nofollow?

Usually not. Links between your own pages help search engines discover them and understand which matter most. The tool flags internal links that carry nofollow so you can decide.

Where does the page get fetched from?

Your browser asks the website directly. Most sites do not allow one website to read another, so the address is then sent to Jina Reader (r.jina.ai), which fetches the page. Only the address is sent. To avoid that, paste the HTML instead.

Why are some links missing?

They are probably added by JavaScript. Turn on Run JavaScript first and check again, or open the page, copy the HTML from your browser’s developer tools and paste it.
