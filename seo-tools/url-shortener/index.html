Using short links safely

  • Check before you click. A short link hides where it goes — paste it into Check a link to see the destination first.
  • Don’t shorten private links. Anyone who has the short link can open it, so keep documents, payment and meeting links out of shorteners — or add a password.
  • Short links can’t be edited. If the page moves you’ll need a new link, so double-check the long URL before you share it widely.
  • Prefer https:// pages. Browsers warn visitors about pages that aren’t encrypted.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is this URL shortener free?

Yes. Shortening, custom endings, QR codes, bulk shortening and click statistics are free, with no sign-up.

How long do the short links last?

They have no expiry date unless you set one or a click limit. The services can remove links that break their rules, such as spam, phishing or malware.

Can I edit or delete a short link?

No. Links made without an account can’t be changed or deleted, so check the long URL before you share it. Removing a link from your list here only hides it in this browser.

Who can see my click statistics?

spoo.me statistics are public to anyone who knows the short link. Set a password to protect both the link and its statistics.

Why are the links not on justtools.online?

JustTools runs entirely in your browser and has no server to store links, so it uses free, open-source shortening services that work straight from the browser.

How can I tell if a short link is safe?

Use Check a link. It follows the link on da.gd’s server and shows every redirect and the final address, with warnings for bare IP addresses, look-alike domains and unencrypted pages. It can’t prove a site is safe, so read the final domain name carefully.
